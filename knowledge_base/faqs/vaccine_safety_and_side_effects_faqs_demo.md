# Vaccine Safety and Side Effects FAQs (DEMO)

> [!NOTE]
> DEMO NOTICE: Strictly labeled as demo clinical data for testing VaxAssist AI RAG search and retrieval.

## Q1: What are the most common normal side effects after vaccination?
**Answer**: Most side effects are mild, transient, and indicate that the immune system is building protection:
- Mild soreness, redness, or swelling at the injection site.
- Low-grade fever (below 38.5°C or 101.3°F) occurring within 24 to 48 hours.
- Mild fussiness, fatigue, or drowsiness in infants.

## Q2: How should post-vaccination fever be managed at home?
**Answer**:
- Keep the child comfortably dressed in lightweight clothing.
- Ensure adequate fluid intake and hydration (breastfeeding or water).
- If the child is uncomfortable or feverish, pediatric paracetamol (acetaminophen) may be administered according to weight and pediatrician instructions.
- Avoid aspirin in children due to the risk of Reye's syndrome.
- Do NOT apply cold ice packs directly to the injection site; a cool damp cloth can be used.

## Q3: When should immediate medical attention be sought?
**Answer**: Contact emergency medical services or visit the nearest emergency clinic if:
- High fever (>39.5°C or 103°F) or fever lasting more than 72 hours.
- Signs of severe allergic reaction (difficulty breathing, facial swelling, widespread hives, rapid heartbeat).
- Inconsolable persistent crying for more than 3 hours or seizure/febrile convulsion.
